## 1. Preamble

Artificial intelligence is no longer theoretical in paramedicine. It is present in
predictive deployment models, routing optimization, documentation support, and
increasingly in clinical decision augmentation. The question is no longer whether AI
will shape paramedic practice. The question is whether the profession will govern how
it does so.

This framework addresses one specific application: AI-assisted patient care report
(PCR) narrative documentation. It is grounded in a broader position on clinical
governance and AI in professionally autonomous paramedic services, published by Nick
Nudell, PhD(c), MS, MPhil, NRP, WP-C, in *Governing Care* (February 2026) and in
the policy literature of The Paramedic Foundation.

The core argument is straightforward. There is a clear ethical distinction between
augmentation and delegation. Using AI to assist documentation is different from
allowing an algorithm to govern patient care. That distinction is where clinical
governance lives.

Technology may evolve rapidly. Professional autonomy and accountability must remain
constant.

## 2. Scope and Purpose

This framework applies to the Paramedic-Narrative skill and any derivative works
produced from it under the CC BY 4.0 license.

The Paramedic-Narrative skill is an editorial documentation tool. It produces draft
PCR narrative text based entirely on information supplied by the provider. It does not
observe patients, assess patients, or make clinical decisions. It does not access
structured PCR fields, vital signs, or medication records directly. It produces a
draft. The provider reviews, corrects, and submits.

The relationship between the provider and this tool is the same relationship that
exists between a clinician and any other documentation aid: the clinician is
responsible for the accuracy, completeness, and professional integrity of the
submitted record.

This tool is intended solely to support paramedics in producing comprehensive, accurate
written documentation of clinical work they have already performed.

---

## 3. The Governing Principle

Artificial intelligence may augment paramedic cognition, pattern recognition, and
documentation. It shall not replace identifiable professional accountability.

Every patient care record influenced by AI must retain clear and traceable clinical
responsibility. The provider who submits the record owns every word in it.

This is not a bureaucratic formality. It is the ethical foundation on which public
trust in paramedicine rests.

> *Automation may assist decision-making. It does not assume responsibility for it.*
>
> -- Nudell, N.G. (2026). Clinical governance in the age of artificial intelligence:
> A profession-wide imperative for paramedicine. *Governing Care.*

## 5. Prohibition on Clinical Decision Use

**This tool must never be used to inform, guide, support, or rationalize clinical
decisions of any kind.**

This prohibition is absolute. It applies regardless of circumstances, time pressure,
or the apparent quality of any AI-generated output.

### Why This Prohibition Exists

Using AI-generated content to drive clinical decisions in paramedicine is:

**Ethically indefensible.** The paramedic's professional and legal authority rests on
personal clinical judgment, applied in real time, within statutory scope of practice.
Delegating that judgment to an AI system -- even partially, even under time pressure
-- violates the foundational accountability structure of the profession. As stated in
the governing position statement: *"Artificial intelligence may inform clinical
reasoning; it does not assume clinical authority."*

**Unsupported by evidence.** This tool has not been tested, validated, or evaluated
for clinical decision support. It has no evidence base for that use. Applying it in
that role is experimentation on patients without consent, without oversight, and
without a safety framework.

**Potentially illegal.** Clinical decision-making by paramedics occurs under statutory
scope-of-practice authority granted to the licensed individual. Introducing an
unvalidated AI system as a decision-making agent may constitute practice outside
authorized scope, unlicensed practice, or deviation from medical director protocols
in ways that carry civil and criminal exposure. No regulatory framework currently
authorizes autonomous AI clinical decision-making in paramedicine.

### What "Clinical Decision" Means in This Context

For the avoidance of doubt, the following are clinical decisions and are not
appropriate uses of this tool:

- Determining whether a patient requires a specific intervention
- Selecting a medication, dose, or route
- Assessing patient acuity or triage priority
- Deciding on transport destination or mode
- Interpreting clinical findings, vital signs, or diagnostic data
- Determining whether a patient requires treatment at all

The tool assists in writing about decisions already made. It does not assist in
making them.

## 6. Privacy and Protected Health Information

Providers using AI documentation tools bear direct responsibility for protecting
patient privacy. The following requirements apply.

### 6.1 PHI Minimization

Enter only the minimum information necessary to produce an accurate narrative draft.
Do not enter patient names, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, addresses, or
other direct identifiers unless your agency's AI use policy and data agreement with
the platform provider explicitly authorize it.

Before using any AI tool for clinical documentation, confirm that your agency has
reviewed the platform's data handling practices for compliance with the Health
Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and applicable state law.

### 6.2 Prohibition on Photographing PHI

**Do not photograph, scan, or upload images of any document, screen, or object that
contains patient-identifying information.** This includes:

- Patient care reports, run sheets, or hospital records
- Medication bottles, prescription labels, or identification cards
- Any document containing a patient's name, date of birth, address, or other
  direct identifier

### 6.3 Prohibition on Photographing Identifiable Images

**Do not photograph or upload images that could identify a patient or their
circumstances.** This includes:

- Images of a patient's face or identifiable physical characteristics
- Vehicle license plates, which may link a patient to an event
- Property, locations, or objects that could identify a specific patient in context

### 6.4 Camera Metadata

Photographs taken with smartphones and digital cameras typically embed metadata
including GPS coordinates, date, time, and device identifier. This metadata can
constitute PHI when associated with a patient encounter. Be aware that uploading
an image to an AI platform may transmit this metadata to a third party.

### 6.5 Responsibility

The Paramedic Foundation is not responsible for privacy breaches resulting from
provider conduct. Providers who photograph or upload PHI to AI platforms do so at
their own professional and legal risk and bear full responsibility for any resulting
HIPAA violation, state privacy breach, or professional discipline.

## 7. Provider Ethical Commitments

By using this tool, you are affirming the following commitments. They are not
enforceable by software. They are professional obligations grounded in the ethical
framework of autonomous paramedic practice.

### 7.1 Full Clinical and Legal Responsibility

The submitted patient care record is your professional document. AI assistance does
not transfer, dilute, or share that responsibility. Every factual claim, every
clinical characterization, every medication entry, and every documented decision in
the submitted record is yours.

If the tool produces an error and you submit it without correction, the error is
yours. This is not a limitation of the tool. It is the correct ethical relationship
between a clinician and a documentation aid.

### 7.2 Review Before Submission

No AI-generated draft is submitted without review. You will read the complete draft.
You will verify every [VERIFY]-tagged item. You will correct any factual error,
clinical mischaracterization, or omission. You will confirm that the narrative
reflects your actual assessment and reasoning -- not a plausible reconstruction of it.

Speed is not a justification for skipping review. A submitted narrative is a
permanent legal record.

### 7.3 No Gap-Filling with AI Output

If you do not know what a finding, value, or event was, the AI does not know either.
The tool is designed to flag missing information and refuse to invent it. Treat that
flag as a stop sign. Do not provide a plausible value to satisfy the tool. Document
only what you directly observed and know.

### 7.4 Controlled Substance Documentation Integrity

The controlled substance audit trail is a legal and regulatory document. No element
of it -- witness identity, drawn amount, wasted amount, chain of custody -- may be
invented, estimated, or reconstructed without direct knowledge. The tool will mark
missing audit elements [VERIFY] and will not fill them in.

Falsification of a controlled substance record is a criminal matter, not a
documentation error.

### 7.5 No Clinical Decision Use

You will not use this tool to determine what you should do, should have done, or
should document as having done. This tool documents decisions you have made using
professional clinical judgment. It does not make decisions. It does not validate
decisions retroactively. If you find yourself using it for either purpose, stop.

### 7.6 Privacy Protection

You will not enter, photograph, or upload patient-identifying information beyond
what is necessary and authorized. You will comply with your agency's AI use policy
and with applicable privacy law. You bear full responsibility for any privacy breach
resulting from your use of this tool.

### 7.7 Disclosure and Compliance

You are responsible for knowing and complying with your agency's policies, your
state's requirements, and any applicable medical director guidance on AI-assisted
documentation. When disclosure of AI tool use is required, you will disclose.

## 8. Documentation Accuracy as a Professional Standard

The submitted patient care record must accurately reflect what the provider observed,
assessed, and did. That obligation is unconditional. It does not change because a
tool assisted in drafting.

AI language models produce plausible text. Plausible is not the same as accurate.
A draft may read fluently and still mischaracterize a finding, omit a clinically
relevant detail, understate or overstate acuity, or apply language that does not
match what the provider actually observed. The provider's review is the only
mechanism that catches these errors before submission.

Review every draft against your direct recollection of the encounter. Correct any
element that does not accurately reflect your findings. The standard is not whether
the draft is reasonable -- it is whether the draft is true.

## 9. Organizational and Professional Governance

Individual ethical commitments are necessary but not sufficient. The profession's
obligation is not merely to use AI tools responsibly at the individual level. It is
to shape the governance frameworks -- regulatory, educational, organizational, and
research-based -- that determine how AI operates across paramedic services.

Governance capacity must mature alongside adoption. AI literacy and critical appraisal
of algorithmic tools are becoming components of professional responsibility, not
elective competencies.

Agencies and medical directors are encouraged to:

- Develop explicit organizational policy on AI documentation tool use
- Include AI tool use in quality assurance and quality improvement review
- Ensure providers understand data handling practices of any AI platform used
- Incorporate AI literacy into continuing education and orientation
- Engage with state and national governance conversations on AI in paramedicine

> *If clinicians don't understand data provenance, confidentiality exposure, model*
> *limitations, or bias, governance won't hold -- no matter how strong the written*
> *policy is. The ethical obligation doesn't move to the tool.*
>
> -- Nudell, N.G. (2026), comment exchange, *Governing Care.*

The Paramedic Foundation is available for consultation on AI governance in
paramedicine systems. Contact: info@paramedicfoundation.org

## 11. Contributing and Submitting Improvements

This is an open, community-maintained professional resource. Providers, agencies,
educators, medical directors, and researchers are encouraged to contribute.

**Ways to contribute:**

- **Clinical content corrections or additions**: If a documentation standard,
  call-type guidance section, or clinical logic item is incorrect or incomplete,
  open a GitHub Issue with the proposed correction and your reasoning.
- **Platform-specific configuration**: If you have developed an agency configuration
  that works well for a specific PCR platform and is de-identified, consider
  contributing it as an example.
- **International adaptations**: Non-US paramedicine systems operate under different
  regulatory frameworks. Adaptations for other jurisdictions are welcome.
- **Research and evaluation**: If you evaluate this tool's outputs in a systematic
  way, share your findings. Evidence about performance informs future versions.

All contributions are subject to review by The Paramedic Foundation before
incorporation. Substantive clinical or policy content changes will be reviewed against
the governing ethical framework and applicable professional standards.

Submit contributions via:
https://github.com/The-Paramedic-Foundation/paramedic-narrative-skill
